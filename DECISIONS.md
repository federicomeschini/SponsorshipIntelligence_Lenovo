# Decision log

Append-only ADR log for choices not fixed by `ARCHITECTURE.md`.

## ADR-0001 — Preserve client exports as immutable raw inputs

- **Context:** The repository initially contained two XLSX survey exports and one CSV exposure export. The architecture requires immutable raw data and reproducible downstream staging.
- **Decision:** Keep the supplied files in their original formats under `data/raw/`, grouped by source, and use descriptive filenames. Connectors may later emit typed parquet to `data/staged/`; raw files must not be edited in place.
- **Alternatives:** Convert the files immediately to parquet; retain the original flat root layout.
- **Consequences:** Source fidelity is preserved and the input provenance is clear. The first ingestion implementation must handle XLSX/CSV and produce manifests and typed staged artifacts.

## ADR-0002 — Keep unresolved client-dependent inputs as templates

- **Context:** The architecture requires event metadata, property taxonomy, ticker mapping, and a royalty schedule, but those inputs were not present in the initial workspace.
- **Decision:** Add schema/header templates with no invented business values. Populate them only from approved source data and log the resulting choice here.
- **Alternatives:** Infer event dates and taxonomies from filenames or external knowledge.
- **Consequences:** The repository is structurally ready while L5/L6/L7 remain correctly blocked until the missing inputs are supplied.

## ADR-0003 — Use NASDAQ-100 converted to HKD as the global technology factor

- **Context:** O-02 requires a reproducible global technology factor. MSCI ACWI IT history generally requires a licensed feed, while NASDAQ-100 and USD/HKD are available through the selected public endpoint.
- **Decision:** Use NASDAQ-100 (`^NDX`) converted to HKD with `HKD=X`; retain Hang Seng (`^HSI`) as the local market factor. Store source prices separately and perform currency conversion in L5.
- **Alternatives:** License MSCI ACWI IT; use unconverted NDX; use a technology ETF.
- **Consequences:** L5 can be prototyped reproducibly. NDX's concentration and US-market tilt must be disclosed, and a licensed factor can later replace it through the same contract.

## ADR-0009 — Activate the automated anonymous_web Trends backend (supersedes ADR-0007's blocked state)

- **Context:** ADR-0007 left D3 blocked, attributing the anonymous endpoint's HTTP 429 to a shared egress IP and requiring 90 manual UI exports. Re-testing from the local residential host showed the 429 was only partly IP-related: the raw `/api/explore` call is rejected because it carries no session cookie, and the `/api/widgetdata/multiline` call throttles intermittently.
- **Decision:** Implement the `anonymous_web` backend already named in `backend_order`. The client seeds the `NID` cookie by first requesting the explore page (which itself may 429 while still setting the cookie), reads the `TIMESERIES` widget token, then fetches the multiline series with bounded retry/back-off. `pull_trends(backend=...)` supports `auto` (manual exports win when present, else anonymous), `manual`, `anonymous`, and `probe`. Raw multiline JSON responses are persisted per draw as immutable provenance.
- **Draw definition (revised):** The original ten-rapid-draws-then-average design does not work against this endpoint: identical requests fired seconds apart return byte-identical cached series, so between-draw variance is zero and the average equals a single pull. We therefore implement W-01's other prescribed mechanism — overlap-ratio window rescaling. Draw 0 is the full-horizon weekly reference; the remaining `draws_per_query - 1` draws are overlapping sub-windows (`window_fraction` of the span, floored so Trends still returns weekly) each rescaled onto the reference by the ratio of summed interest over their unsuppressed overlap (`min_overlap_weeks` guard). Stacking the rescaled draws makes `interest_mean` robust to any single normalising peak week, and `interest_sd` now measures genuine normalisation instability rather than sampling noise.
- **Alternatives:** Keep requiring 90 manual exports; wait for official closed-alpha credentials; adopt `pytrends` or a paid scraper; keep rapid-repeat draws (rejected — degenerate variance); add a fine 90-day daily granularity splice (deferred — weekly is sufficient for L1).
- **Consequences:** A W-01-compliant curated worldwide Trends series is produced automatically, without manual exports or API credentials. `interest_sd` is small where a query's worldwide salience is normalisation-stable and wider for spiky event-driven co-brand queries, which is the intended diagnostic. The series still depends on an unofficial endpoint that Google may change or throttle, so the manual-export path and queue remain the audited fallback. The manual parser recognises English UI headers only; non-English exports (e.g. an Italian `Settimana` export) must be re-exported in English or ingested via the automated backend.

- **Context:** A review of `queries_trends.yaml` against the LOCKED §3.3 family objectives found the families structurally present but three adequacy gaps: (1) the category-purchase family used bare product names (`ThinkPad`, `Lenovo Legion`) that proxy salience, not the consideration/intent the commercial pillar requires; (2) no synthetic-control donor queries existed, though O-05/L6 require competitor Trends series; (3) the brand family's `target_pillar` was `awareness`, which is not one of the five `survey_pillars`.
- **Decision:**
  - **Category-purchase:** retain the three product-name queries as candidates and add commercial-intent modifiers (`Lenovo laptop price`, `ThinkPad price`) so the family genuinely proxies intent per the §3.3 example.
  - **Competitor donor family (new, `role: scm_donor`):** add Dell, Asus, Acer (clean computing brands, bare term), plus `HP laptop` and `Samsung laptop` (raw terms ambiguous / phone-dominated → PC-line per O-05) and `Huawei` (flagged `quality_flag: low` for phone/telecom and CN-brand coverage per W-09). These are candidates for L6 donor screening, not Lenovo funnel-stage proxies; the `role` field lets §5.2 validation skip them.
  - **Brand stage label:** ~~change the prior from `awareness` to `media`~~ **superseded by ADR-0011** — pillars were removed; the brand family's `target_stage` is `awareness` (a funnel stage). All final stage assignment remains empirical (§5.2).
  - **Co-branded family:** left unchanged. The `Lenovo X` conjunction is the correct construct for property-attributed salience; broadening to bare property terms would measure the property's own salience, not Lenovo's. The `esports` property (named in D2) is not added because no client-confirmed co-branded term exists (ADR-0002 — no invented business values).
- **Alternatives:** Replace product-name queries entirely (rejected — they are valid candidates); build donors as bare brand terms (rejected — ambiguity per O-05); pull donors in a separate config (rejected — same W-01 treatment needed, one config is simpler).
- **Consequences:** The query set grows from 9 to 17 (brand 1, category 5, co-branded 5, donor 6); the pull produces 170 draws. `composition_id` stays `worldwide_v1` (geo composition unchanged). Downstream proxy validation (§5.2) and any funnel-stage/property consumer must filter on `role` so donor and outcome series are not conflated. Co-branded property salience remains sparse (most weeks `<1`), a documented measurement limit on the L4b identification core that compounds with the unavailable Blinkfire engagement rate (ADR-0005); whether property salience is measurable in Trends at all is a client-facing decision, not resolved here.

## ADR-0011 — Replace the five brand pillars with the client's marketing funnel; construct a non-canonical wave anchor from the client waves

- **Context:** The architecture assumed the D1 survey delivered a composite plus five brand *pillars* (media, digital, institutional, commercial, experience). Inspection of the actual client waves shows this is wrong and will never hold: the client measures a marketing **funnel**. The two client survey exports are both D1 waves — the GWI export provides population-level quarterly **engagement** and **consideration** (Q1 2017–Q1 2026), and the consumer-research crosstabs provide **awareness**, **appeal**, and **purchase_intent** (segmented by sponsorship awareness, 2024–2026, with a 2024→2025 response-scale break). The assumed composite+pillar analytical extract is not coming.
- **Decision (funnel replaces pillars, everywhere):** The canonical brand construct is the ordered funnel `awareness → engagement → appeal → consideration → purchase_intent`. `survey_pillars` becomes `survey_funnel` (base.yaml); `PILLARS`/`target_pillar` become `FUNNEL_STAGES`/`target_stage` in code and config; ARCHITECTURE §3.2/§3.3/§4/§5.2, the reports, and the anchor template are reworded to funnel stages. `composite` is retained only as an optional overall roll-up (no longer a PCA-of-pillars); the anchor no longer requires it. This supersedes the pillar terminology in ADR-0006 and ADR-0010 and the pillar model in the architecture.
- **Decision (constructed anchor):** Because no approved analytical extract exists, `survey_anchor.py` gains `construct_wave_anchor`, which builds a **non-canonical** anchor from the client waves: the GWI quarterly series populate `engagement` and `consideration`; fieldwork dates are set to quarter boundaries with the midpoint as the anchor point (a documented approximation of GWI's continuous fielding); `awareness`/`appeal`/`purchase_intent` are left null in the population anchor because the crosstab values are sponsorship-segmented and scale-broken, not population wave scores. Every row carries `canonical_anchor_eligible: false`, and a deviations register records each departure from the anchor contract. The canonical path (`ingest_survey_anchor`) and its blocked status remain for when the client supplies an approved extract.
- **Alternatives:** Adopt GWI directly as the index target with no anchor artifact (rejected — less reusable, no deviations trail); fold the crosstab awareness/appeal/intent into population scores (rejected — they are segment-conditioned and scale-broken, so that would fabricate population values); keep pillars and wait (rejected — they do not exist).
- **Consequences:** L2 can prototype on a usable-but-caveated two-stage funnel anchor (engagement, consideration) now, with three stages pending a proper population extract. Any index/attribution built on the constructed anchor must carry the non-canonical caveat and must not be presented as the client-approved Brand Index. The funnel is an ordered sequence, not a factor structure, so the architecture's PCA-composite and LMG-attribution specifics are superseded and will be redefined when L2 is built.

## ADR-0012 — Consumer-research crosstab enters as a sponsorship-lift companion, not as a population index anchor

- **Context:** We tested whether the consumer-research crosstab (awareness/appeal/purchase_intent, split by FIFA-sponsorship Aware/Unaware, per property, 2024–2026, scale-broken) could populate the population funnel anchor. It has no weighted population total, and the guardrail forbids recombining segments with the unweighted `Column n`. A GWI-calibrated weight was attempted — solve `w` so `w·aware + (1−w)·unaware` matches the GWI population level — but it is **degenerate**: implied `w` is negative in every wave (−0.90, −0.17, −0.24) because GWI general-population Lenovo consideration (~11%) sits below even the sponsorship-unaware FIFA-context intent (~18–37%). The constructs and contexts are not on a shared scale, so no valid population total can be recovered.
- **Decision:** Keep the GWI stages (engagement, consideration) as the population brand-index anchor. Do **not** fold the crosstab into the population anchor. Instead add a companion artifact, `sponsorship_funnel_lift`, = `aware − unaware` per funnel stage × property × wave. Differencing cancels the within-wave response scale, so the lift is comparable within a wave even where the levels are not. Both segments are retained in the artifact.
- **Alternatives:** Unaware-segment-as-baseline (rejected — discards the aware signal); unweighted midpoint (rejected — fabricates a ~50%-aware assumption); GWI-calibrated weight (rejected — degenerate, see Context).
- **Consequences:** The index stays a clean population measure while the sponsorship dimension is preserved as an explicit lift layer. The lift is **observational, not causal**: sponsorship-aware respondents self-select, so the gap upper-bounds the true effect (causal estimation is the job of the L4b panel / event study / SCM stack, W-04); the artifact is flagged `is_causal_effect: false`. The **awareness** lift is near-mechanical (the aware segment is ~100% brand-aware by construction); the **appeal** (+33 to +48pp) and **purchase_intent** (+28 to +41pp) lifts are the substantive signals.

## ADR-0013 — Single Brand Index: PCA composite (Tier A) + state-space signal fusion (Tier B); reinstates a composite

- **Context:** ADR-0011 deferred a composite, arguing the funnel is an ordered sequence, not a factor structure. That was too dismissive: a brand index is conventionally a single value, and the funnel stages are highly correlated (they move together), so a first principal component is a well-behaved composite. A single index is required for the L1→L7 spine.
- **Decision (Tier A composite):** `srmp/index/composite.py` aggregates the populated funnel stages into one quarterly Brand Index via PC1 of the standardized stages, oriented so higher = stronger, on a mean-100/sd-15 scale. With the two GWI population stages, PC1 loadings are 0.71/0.71 and explain 98.7% of variance (≈ standardized average); it becomes a genuine multi-stage PCA when awareness/appeal/purchase_intent gain population extracts.
- **Decision (Tier B final weekly index = state-space fusion):** `srmp/index/statespace.py` (the architecture's `v2_statespace`) is the final weekly index. A single latent weekly brand state follows a random walk; every signal is a standardized noisy observation `obs = x_t + e`, with observation variance encoding trust — **survey waves are the priority signal (var 0.08), admitted proxies are secondary (var 0.40, ~5× looser)**. The proxy variance is tuned so the weekly Trends texture actually passes through between waves (an earlier 20× ratio over-smoothed the index into quarterly interpolation with no weekly movement); at 5× the survey still pins the level while the proxy drives the weekly path. A Kalman filter + RTS smoother fuses them: the index is pinned at survey-wave weeks (SE ≈ 2.5) and follows the admitted Trends proxies between waves (visible weekly movement, wider band). This subsumes the Tier-A composite (loadings aggregate the stages) and Chow-Lin (temporal fill) in one coherent model. Chow-Lin (`v1_chowlin`) is retained as the simpler single-indicator alternative (consideration built, pass-through error 3e-17).
- **Signals fused (level index):** GWI engagement + consideration and consumer-research **awareness** (unaware baseline, consistent binary scale) as priority; the mean-z of the six admitted Trends proxies as the secondary high-frequency signal. **Appeal and purchase_intent are excluded from the level fusion** because their 2024→2025 scale break makes levels non-comparable; they remain in the sponsorship-lift companion (ADR-0012) and will re-enter when consistent-scale versions exist.
- **Alternatives:** Disaggregate each stage separately then combine (rejected — composite-then-disaggregate is cleaner and matches Tier A); equal-weight average (kept implicitly — PC1≈average at two stages); admit all proxies regardless of the gate (rejected — P4; wrong-sign Wikipedia/GDELT stay out).
- **Consequences:** A working, provisional single weekly Brand Index exists (2022–2026, 235 weeks), honestly uncertainty-banded and anchored to the client's survey waves. It is **non-canonical** (GWI-based anchor, ADR-0011) and must not be presented as the client-approved Brand Index. When the approved analytical extract arrives, the same pipeline runs canonically by swapping the anchor. *(Signal construction and tuning superseded by ADR-0014 — the var-0.40/hand-tuned-q parameterisation and the awareness signal produced a mis-levelled index.)*

## ADR-0014 — Level-coherent state-space index: full-history standardization, proxy level calibration, MLE process variance; awareness excluded from the level fusion

- **Context:** The v2 state-space index contradicted its own anchor. At the 2026Q1 wave week it read **121.5** (peaking at **140.5** in May 2026, z ≈ +2.7 "all-time high") while the Tier A composite — the survey itself — read **92.2** (below the long-run average). Four construction defects compounded: **(1)** survey stages were z-scored *after* truncating to the 2022+ grid window, so the level relative to the full 2017–2026 anchor was destroyed (2026Q1, below the long-run mean, became the *top* of the depressed window); **(2)** the proxy composite entered in its own-history z-units with loading 1, so its arbitrary level/amplitude steered the index — the June-2026 World Cup search surge (proxy z +3.3) was read as a brand-*health* boom; **(3)** the process variance q was hand-fixed at ~0.3 (the ADR-0013 "texture" tuning), so survey information decayed within a week or two and wave weeks became one-week spikes; **(4)** the crosstab awareness signal averaged FWC/FWWC/CWC unaware-awareness per week (its movement was mostly *which property was surveyed*: FWC ~0.52–0.53 vs FWWC ~0.38–0.45) and z-scored ≤5 points on their own sd, so a 1.4pp move entered as ±1σ with survey-grade trust. The published artifact also recorded parameters (proxy var 0.25, q 0.3) the code could not produce — an unreproducible ad-hoc run.
- **Decision:** `srmp/index/statespace.py` rebuilt for level coherence. **(a)** Survey stages standardized over the **full anchor history**, then sliced to the grid — the weekly index now shares the Tier A composite's mean-100/sd-15 meaning. **(b)** The proxy composite is **affine-calibrated onto the anchor scale** by OLS of wave-week survey z on proxy z (17 waves; fitted slope 0.11, so a +3σ salience spike moves the level ~0.33σ); its observation variance is the calibration residual variance floored at 1.5× the survey variance; a proxy with thin overlap or non-positive slope is excluded. **(c)** q chosen by **MLE over a bounded grid** (selected 1e-4, interior), so anchor information propagates between waves. **(d)** The crosstab awareness signal is **excluded from the level fusion** (composition artifact + own-sample scale; it stays in the sponsorship-lift companion, ADR-0012) — it may re-enter when a per-property series has enough waves (≥6) on a consistent scale, calibrated like the proxy. Artifacts regenerated via the CLI so manifest and code agree; the manifest now records the calibration and the q-selection mode.
- **Alternatives:** Keep window standardization and rescale the output (rejected — hides the defect, still incoherent with Tier A); keep hand-tuned q for visible weekly "texture" (rejected — the texture was fabricated level movement; the honest weekly path is smooth because the proxy demonstrably carries little level information); drop the proxy entirely (rejected — calibrated, it contributes honest between-wave information and sets the grid span).
- **Consequences:** At every survey wave week the v2 index now sits within 3 points of the Tier A composite (mean gap 1.3); spring 2026 reads ~90–91 (below long-run average, slowly recovering), not 140. Weekly movement between waves is small — that is the honest message: the admitted search proxies explain little of the anchor's level variation, so the weekly index is mostly smooth interpolation with narrow bands. A regression test (`test_statespace_level_coheres_with_tier_a_composite`) now fails the build if the two Brand Index artifacts diverge at wave weeks. Any report or figure citing the previous 120–140 levels for 2026 must be regenerated.

  The Chow–Lin and state-space builds attempted to manufacture weekly brand
  equity by forcing sparse, non-canonical survey observations through latent
  interpolation models. A later proxy-led build pooled parent-brand and product
  queries, but that pooled signal failed a four-quarter temporal holdout against

  `v4_proxy_led_brand_calibrated`. Worldwide Trends for the exact `Lenovo`

  engagement/consideration PC1 supplies only orientation and display-scale

## ADR-0016 - Treat the Lenovo Brand Index as descriptive, not a forecast model

- **Context:** ADR-0015 used a last-four-quarter forecast-style holdout as a publication gate. That is the wrong criterion for the current purpose: the weekly Lenovo Brand Index is a descriptive construct, not a model intended to forecast a future GWI wave. The gate risked selecting components for short-horizon extrapolation rather than construct validity. It also obscured the meaningful choice between a survey-neutral PCA composite and an evidence-weighted composite.
- **Decision:** Forecast or nowcast exercises may be retained as separate research diagnostics, but cannot accept, reject, or select C-INDEX. The current canonical simple index remains the parent-brand Lenovo Trends salience series, GWI-calibrated only for orientation and display scale. Two Lenovo-only multi-signal alternatives are documented: PCA (common movement across salience, product portfolio, commercial intent, and earned-news tone) and evidence weighting (non-negative, sum-to-one ridge weights fitted to the same four dimensions against GWI). Evidence weighting uses a fixed ridge penalty of 5.0 as a strong-shrinkage governance rule, not a forecast-tuned hyperparameter. All three methods report survey convergence and leave-one-quarter-out weight sensitivity.
- **Alternatives:** Continue to choose the index by temporal holdout (rejected: it changes the target from measurement to forecast); make GWI a weekly anchor (rejected: sparse waves do not measure every week); include peer position (rejected: benchmarking is not Lenovo brand performance).
- **Consequences:** Current manifests contain no forecast pass/fail field. A non-positive calibration slope, unstable signs, or material dependence on a single GWI quarter triggers review before publication. The crosstab survey remains a sponsorship-lift companion, not a population calibration target. This supersedes ADR-0015's temporal-holdout validation and publication gate.

## ADR-0017 - Isolate the sponsorship-to-monetization bridge as an experimental path

- **Context:** The Brand Index, observational aware-minus-unaware funnel lift, and confirmed Blinkfire impressions/views can now be connected conceptually, but the workspace is not a Git repository and the evidence is insufficient for a causal exposure-lift regression or monetary ROI. Only five 2025-plus property-wave observations have comparable survey scales and preceding exposure coverage. The approved royalty schedule, attributable Brand Index change, brand-attributable revenue, fee, discount rate, and persistence horizon are absent.
- **Decision:** Implement `sponsorship_monetization_v1` under isolated experimental code, config, and curated-output namespaces. It cannot replace C-INDEX, C-EXPOSURE, or C-ROI. Appeal and purchase-intent lift are averaged within property-wave; awareness is excluded as mechanically related to segment definition. Impressions receive fixed scenario adstock delta 0.80 and are aligned over the 13 weeks ending at reported month-end because exact fieldwork dates are unavailable. The property allocation scenario is proportional to each property's mean across survey waves of trailing-adstock exposure times positive funnel lift. This share may distribute an independently identified total effect but cannot create or identify that effect. Monetary rows are emitted only if every explicit valuation input is populated.
- **Alternatives:** Put survey lift into Brand Index weights (rejected: different construct and selected segments); estimate a fixed-effects exposure-lift model on five observations (rejected: not credible); populate valuation inputs with assumed business values (rejected: commercially indefensible); allocate using full-period exposure (rejected: leaks later exposure into earlier survey evidence).
- **Consequences:** A reproducible attribution bridge and executable royalty-relief gate now exist. Current property shares are scenario diagnostics, not causal value shares. No monetary result is produced in the current run, and canonical artifacts remain unchanged. The experiment must be re-estimated when exact fieldwork dates, additional comparable waves, broadcast exposure, a defensible counterfactual Brand Index change, and approved valuation inputs become available.

## ADR-0018 - Implement but do not accept the first synthetic-control attribution candidate

- **Context:** Five base donor Trends series cover the complete pre/post period, so an experimental Lenovo synthetic control can be fitted around the 2024-10-15 FIFA partnership announcement. A positive point estimate is not sufficient for monetization; it must be distinctive relative to donor and time placebos. The royalty schedule remains pending with no approved Index/rate points.
- **Decision:** Fit non-negative sum-to-one donor weights with all standardization frozen before the first full post week, 2024-10-21. Use Dell, Asus, Acer, HP laptop, and Samsung laptop as base donors; keep Huawei sensitivity-only. Convert the synthetic gap to canonical Index points, report unweighted and FIFA-digital-exposure-weighted gaps, and run donor, fake-date, leave-one-donor-out, and Huawei-inclusion diagnostics. The monetization bridge may use the candidate automatically only when the experimental pre-fit and placebo screens pass and configuration explicitly opts in. Implement a piecewise-linear royalty resolver requiring at least two approved Index/decimal-rate points and prohibit extrapolation.
- **Result:** Pre RMSPE is 0.305 Index points and the exposure-weighted candidate is +0.892 points, with donor-set range +0.618 to +1.431. The donor-placebo p-value is 0.833, so the candidate status is `candidate_not_distinguishable`; it is not used for valuation. The royalty resolver is operational but blocked because `config/royalty_schedule.yaml` has status `pending_client_calibration` and zero points.
- **Consequences:** The counterfactual estimate is preserved as sensitivity evidence, not a causal central case. Monetary output remains empty. Accepting a non-zero Index attribution requires stronger donor/placebo evidence or an alternative identified design; resolving royalty bps requires an approved schedule or external comparable-royalty calibration.

## ADR-0019 - Make the World Cup the primary prospective sponsorship evaluation

- **Context:** The greatest Lenovo sponsorship impact may occur during the World Cup, whose complete exposure, post-event Index, survey, and persistence data are not yet available. The 2024-announcement/FCWC synthetic control is an interim diagnostic and fails its donor-placebo screen. Waiting to choose the World Cup method after observing the outcome would create researcher degrees of freedom around dates, donors, transformations, and windows.
- **Decision:** Preregister `world_cup_impact_v1` now as the primary sponsorship impact evaluation. Freeze an exposure-weighted actual-minus-counterfactual Brand Index gap as the primary estimand, with at least thirteen post-tournament weeks; keep all-post, peak-four-week, persistence, co-brand search, and survey lift as secondary outcomes. Require jointly scaled anchored Trends panels, at least nine credible donors, 104 pre weeks with a final 26-week validation holdout, augmented SCM with simplex SCM and synthetic-DiD robustness, product-launch controls, donor/time placebos, and placebo p-value at most 0.10. Prohibit post-outcome tuning. Approved event dates remain null until supplied rather than inferred from outcome peaks.
- **Alternatives:** Treat the 2024 announcement as the final intervention (rejected: likely misses the largest activation); reuse the current five independently normalized donors (rejected: weak placebo resolution and measurement coherence); tune the model after World Cup data arrive (rejected: biases attribution); require broadcast data before any counterfactual estimate (rejected: broadcast is essential for total-exposure interpretation but not logically required to estimate an Index gap).

## ADR-0029 - Implement the preregistered World Cup estimator, with confounder controls

- **Context:** `world_cup_impact_v1` was a readiness skeleton with no estimator. A first interim run on mixed data showed a -1.2 point tournament gap that coincided with an all-brand fall in search salience. Diagnostics showed three contaminations: (1) a category-wide surge in PC-brand searches (+60-100% YoY, late March to mid-May 2026, consistent with the memory-shortage price rush) unwinding through the tournament, with Lenovo loading 1.18x on category swings (1.15 on 2023-2025 alone); (2) the July Lenovo pull understated its own last weeks by about 15 log points against the October re-pull, while the donors came from October; (3) the trailing weeks of every pull are provisional.

Ridge penalty: multiplier x training weeks, chosen on an inner 26-week pre-period split, with boundary selection flagged. Donor pool: registry `base` (11). Treatment: approved `activation_start`, else the tournament-start week flagged provisional. Estimands follow the preregistration (exposure-weighted window gap, all-post, peak four weeks, persistence, co-brand search as a descriptive level change); weeks without observed Blinkfire carry no weight. Exclude the last two weeks of every pull. Add, explicitly *after* partial outcomes were seen and therefore robustness-only, a generalized synthetic control (interactive fixed effects, Xu 2017) whose Lenovo-specific factor loadings absorb differential sensitivity to category shocks, with blocked cross-validation for the factor count and its own placebos. Every run writes confounder diagnostics (pre-trend, same window in prior years, donor co-movement, leave-one-donor-out, YoY category-loading residual) that never alter the estimate.
- **Result:** On matched October data (15 window weeks, 9 of 13 persistence weeks): tournament-weeks mean gap +0.01 (ASCM), +0.00 (simplex), -0.03 (SDID), -0.25 (factor model); exposure-weighted primary +0.25 (range +0.07 to +0.25 across methods); post-tournament mean +0.51 to +0.68. - **Consequences:** The interim tournament effect on brand salience is approximately zero and not distinguishable from donor placebos; the post-tournament drift is positive but the window is incomplete. Status remains `interim_estimate_gates_not_met` (activation date, index coverage to 2026-10-18, Blinkfire after 2026-07-20, 13 persistence weeks, placebo p). ## ADR-0031 - Owner-selected valuation: brand and sponsorship share of price formation (dominance analysis)

- **Context:** No valuation route has an identified brand-to-money link (ADR-0030). The owner chose to value the brand as its relative importance in Lenovo's share-price formation: if the brand contributes x% of price formation, it is worth x% of company value. The owner also needs a coefficient for the sponsorship effect. The known objections were raised and recorded before the choice. A variance share is not a value share, the share depends on the other regressors, and dominance weights are sign-blind.
- **Decision:** Implement `brand_value_dominance_v1`. The outcome is weekly Lenovo log returns (P2). Regressors are grouped by rule from `stock_brand_response_v2`: market (HSI, lagged Nasdaq-100 in HKD), category demand, Lenovo events (results, campaigns, promotions, mixed events) and brand (Brand Index innovation). Group general dominance (Shapley-Owen) splits the full-model R2. Each single-regressor group's share is signed by its full-model coefficient, so co-movement in the wrong direction is not counted as value. Brand value = signed brand share of explained variance x company value. For the sponsorship, post-announcement brand innovations are split into the synthetic-control-implied sponsorship part and the remainder (provisional trailing weeks excluded); sponsorship value = its signed share x company value. The coefficient is sponsorship value divided by the v3 sustained lift (US$ per sustained Index point), which can be applied to any measured lift, including the World Cup estimate. Company value is the mean post-announcement market capitalisation (ADR-0030 rule), with the pre-announcement and latest capitalisations as sensitivities. Uncertainty comes from an 8-week moving-block bootstrap (1,000 replications). Stability is checked across sample windows and leave-one-group-out.
- **Result:** Full sample (164 weeks, R2 0.175): brand coefficient +0.46% return per innovation point (robust SE 0.85%), share 0.42% of explained variance, brand value US$90m (90% bootstrap -US$1.10bn to +US$2.17bn; stability range US$40m-706m). Post-announcement split (100 weeks, R2 0.172): sponsorship coefficient +0.80% (SE 1.19%), signed share +2.54%, so sponsorship value US$539m (total-variance lower bound US$93m; 90% bootstrap -US$656m to +US$3.76bn). The other brand innovations carry -1.23% (SE 1.88%), a signed share of -12.1%. **Coefficient: US$624m per sustained Index point** (90% bootstrap -US$759m to +US$4,347m). Applied to the interim World Cup lift (+0.25) that gives US$157m. With the latest capitalisation as base, the sponsorship value would be US$1.44bn; with the pre-announcement base, US$443m.
- **Consequences:** This is the operational sponsorship coefficient by owner decision. It is not causal or accounting value, and its interval includes zero and negative values, so it must be quoted with the interval and the stability range. Brand and sponsorship values come from different samples and models and are not additive: the sponsorship value exceeds the full-sample brand value because the other post-announcement brand variation is negatively related to returns. A refresh can move the coefficient materially; it inherits the attribution gate status of `sponsorship_counterfactual_v1` (currently not distinguishable).

## ADR-0032 - Value the sponsorship through its Brand Index lift (indirect chain), not a direct link

- **Context:** ADR-0031 also valued the sponsorship directly, through its own share of return variance. The owner clarified the intended logic: estimate the general effect of the Brand Index on price formation (brand value), then value the sponsorship by how much it lifted the Index, with no direct sponsorship-to-stock link. Index points are on a 100/15 display scale, so a lift divided by the Index level would not be a meaningful share of the brand.
- **Decision:** In `brand_value_dominance_v1` the primary result becomes a three-step chain. (1) Brand value (BV) = the signed brand share of explained weekly return variance x the mean post-announcement market capitalisation (ADR-0031 specification). (2) The proxy-led Index is exactly affine in raw `brand_lenovo` search interest, which has a true zero. The sustained synthetic-control lift therefore converts to a ratio-scale salience uplift = lift / (counterfactual Index level - zero-interest Index level). The map is re-fitted on every run and fails if the correlation drops below 0.999. (3) Incremental BV = BV x salience uplift x the elasticity of brand value to salience, set to 1 (proportional) as an explicit assumption. The coefficient is the incremental BV per Index point, applicable to any measured lift. The ADR-0031 direct split is kept only as `diagnostic_direct_link_not_used`. Stability adds the Index change instead of the innovation as the brand measure.
- **Result:** Index = 80.61 + 0.2034 x search interest (r = 1.000), so zero interest corresponds to 80.61. The post-announcement counterfactual sits at 90.11 (interest 46.7), and the +0.865 sustained lift is a **+9.1% salience uplift**. BV = 0.42% x US$21.2bn = US$90m, so **incremental BV = US$8.2m and the coefficient is US$9.5m per Index point**. The 90% bootstrap for incremental BV is -US$101m to +US$198m; across the leave-one-donor-out lift range it is US$7.6-12.5m; across specifications it is US$3.7-64m (US$26m with the Index change as the brand measure, US$64m on the pre-announcement sample). The interim World Cup lift (+0.25) gives US$2.4m. On the latest market capitalisation base, incremental BV would be US$22m.
- **Consequences:** Steps 2 and 3 are well defined and independent of how BV is obtained. Step 1 dominates the answer and the uncertainty: weekly Brand Index news explains little of Lenovo's return variance, so BV is far below third-party brand valuations, which typically put tech-hardware brands at 10-40% of enterprise value. A third-party or survey-driver brand value can replace step 1 without changing steps 2-3. The chain inherits the unaccepted attribution status of `sponsorship_counterfactual_v1`.

## ADR-0033 - Brand value by discounted branded earnings (ISO 10668 income approach)

- **Context:** The dominance-based brand value (ADR-0031) applied a variance share to market capitalisation and gave US$90m, against Brand Finance's US$5.66bn (2025). The owner asked for a proper DCF using branded earnings rather than a royalty rate: the brand's share of share-price formation is assumed to equal its share of economic profit.
- **Decision:** Add `brand_value_dcf_v1`, an ISO 10668 income-approach valuation using the brand-earnings-split method. The base is FY25/26 as reported (revenue US$83.075bn, operating profit US$3.262bn, profit before tax US$2.670bn, tax US$0.510bn). Invested capital is total equity + total debt - cash - short-term investments at 31 March 2026 (US$8.661bn), and it grows with revenue. Revenue growth starts at the trailing three-fiscal-year CAGR and fades linearly to 3% by year 5. Economic profit = NOPAT - WACC x opening invested capital. Branded earnings = economic profit x role of brand. These are discounted at a USD CAPM WACC: 10-year Treasury yield; Blume-adjusted 104-week beta against ACWI; ERP 5.0%; debt at risk-free + 150bp after tax; market-value weights. A Gordon terminal value is added. The literal ADR-0031 share of price formation is the default role of brand. The firm-specific share (the brand's share among the non-market driver groups, i.e. Lenovo-specific price formation) is reported alongside. Sponsorship value follows ADR-0032. ACWI and the 10-year Treasury yield (^TNX) are added to the market connector, and a sourced `lenovo_annual_financials.csv` is added to reference data.
- **Result:** Operating margin is 3.93%, tax rate 19.1%, ROIC 30.5%. Revenue growth starts at 10.3%. WACC is 10.06% (risk-free 5.30%, beta 1.03 from raw 1.05, cost of equity 10.47%, debt weight 8.3%). Economic profit runs from US$2.04bn in year 1 to US$2.47bn in year 5, and its present value is US$30.9bn. At the literal role of 0.42%, brand value is US$131m, incremental sponsorship value US$12m and the coefficient US$13.8m per Index point. At the firm-specific role of 6.40%, brand value is **US$1.98bn** (US$1.56-2.62bn across WACC +/-1pp and g +/-0.5pp), incremental sponsorship value **US$180m**, the coefficient **US$208m per Index point**, and the World Cup interim lift is worth US$52m. Brand Finance's US$5.66bn implies a role of brand of 18.3%.
- **Consequences:** The DCF mechanics and inputs are sourced and reproducible. The role of brand dominates the result, scaling it one-for-one. The firm-specific definition is the like-for-like measure, because economic profit is Lenovo-specific while global market factors explain about 93% of return variance; adopting it as primary is the owner's decision. ISO 10668 expects the brand's contribution to be supported by a behavioural analysis of demand drivers. Here the share-price relative-importance proxy stands in for it and must be disclosed as such. Incremental sponsorship value carries the proportional-salience assumption and the unaccepted attribution status.

## ADR-0034 - Select the announcement counterfactual design on pre-period evidence only

- **Context:** The announcement synthetic control (ADR-0021) shows a positive, growing gap (+0.16 after 26 weeks, +0.49 after 52, +0.85 overall) but fails its donor-placebo test after the October 2026 refresh (p = 2/12; MacBook and BenQ diverge more). Improving the design after seeing which placebos beat Lenovo risks tuning the design until the test passes.
- **Decision:** Add `announcement_design_selection_v1`, which runs in two phases. Phase A receives only weeks before 2024-10-21 and scores 12 candidate designs: four methods (simplex SCM, ASCM, synthetic DiD, generalized SCM factor model) x three donor pools (registry base; base plus the sensitivity donors Huawei and MSI; base donors whose pre-period gap to Lenovo has no linear trend, |HAC t| < 2). Each design is scored by rolling-origin out-of-sample error (four 16-week folds from week 82). The lowest error wins, with a fixed tie-break, and Phase A writes a selection record asserting that no post-period outcome was accessed. Phase B fits the selected design once. The primary inference is unchanged from v1: two-sided post/pre RMSPE ratio, no placebo filter, all post weeks except the two provisional ones. A specification curve reports every design x window x placebo filter (none, 2x, 5x pre-RMSPE) x statistic (ratio, one-sided mean gap / pre-RMSPE).
- **Result:** Phase A selected synthetic DiD on the 13-donor pool (out-of-sample RMSPE 0.308 Index points; ASCM on the base pool 0.313; simplex 0.322). Every design under-predicts Lenovo out of sample by about +0.19 before the announcement. Only 5 of 11 base donors pass the parallel-trend screen, and that pool predicts worst (0.349-0.389). Phase B: the lift is **+1.15 Index points**, but placebo **p = 0.29** (BenQ 5.01, MacBook 4.58, Asus 3.67 and Huawei 3.30 against Lenovo's 3.37 ratio), so it fails. Excluding the spring 2026 surge gives +0.78 (p 0.29-0.43); stopping before it gives +0.61 (p 0.43-0.50). Across all 216 specifications the lift is positive in every one (+0.27 to +1.82), but p <= 0.10 in only 1.4% of them. The single passing design, synthetic DiD on the base pool (p = 0.083), was not the pre-selected one and is not claimed. The pre-RMSPE filters removed no placebo. On the level-shift statistic MacBook (+3.27), BenQ (+3.06) and Asus (+2.28) show sustained drifts as large as Lenovo's.
- **Consequences:** The announcement-period salience lift is robustly positive in direction and size (about +0.6 to +1.2 points, roughly +6-12%). Over a two-year horizon it cannot be separated from ordinary relative drift among consumer-tech brands with an 11-13 donor pool. Better tuning will not change this. Power must come from more screened donors, shorter registered windows (the World Cup persistence window) or outcomes with distinctive signal (co-brand association, survey lift), each needing its own placebo design.

## ADR-0039 - Brand-level Lenovo Brand Index as a mixed-frequency factor model (experimental)

- **Context:** The canonical Brand Index is a rescaled count of worldwide Google searches for "Lenovo" (affine in search interest), so it moves with the whole PC market. The owner asked for a proper brand index: weekly, using the quarterly GWI survey as much as possible, weighted by factor analysis, accepting that the synthetic comparison may lack some components. A first build with every available Lenovo signal failed. Wikipedia page views and GDELT news volume and tone moved against the survey, and survey-only years before 2022 flipped the survey loadings negative. Factor analysis then gave about 99% of the weight to product and price searches ("ThinkPad", "… price"). These searches measure PC demand and step up 3–7× in 2025Q3, an increase also visible in Google's single full-horizon request. That index read about 250 against 100 before.
- **Decision:** Build the index at the **brand level**. Each weekly signal is Lenovo's share of attention against rival brands, log(Lenovo) − log(sum of rivals). Google share of search uses the joint common-anchor panel with the registry's `base` rivals as the denominator. Wikipedia share uses English page views of the Lenovo article against Asus, Dell and HP Inc. A signal enters only if its quarterly mean correlates positively with both GWI series in levels and in changes (P4). One weekly AR(1) factor is estimated by maximum likelihood and the Kalman smoother. Each GWI wave enters as a measurement of the factor's quarterly average, with no interpolation. The sample starts in 2022, and a 0.05 noise-variance floor applies. The display scale is 100 = pre-announcement average, with the factor mapped into share-of-search log units, so 110 = brand share about 10% above that level. Product and price searches are written as a descriptive `product_search_demand` column, outside the index. Sensitivities: every weekly signal forced in; survey removed. Code: `srmp/index/brand_factor.py`, `config/brand_index_factor.yaml`, outputs `data/curated/index/brand_index_factor_*`. For the effect, `sponsorship_total_effect_v1` gains `outcome_column` and `donor_share_of_search`. Each donor is put on the same basis, its own log share against the base rivals without itself. Lenovo is in no denominator, so a sponsorship lift cannot depress the donors. Two configs: `sponsorship_total_effect_factor_index_v1` (factor index outcome; indicative, because rivals have no survey) and `sponsorship_total_effect_share_of_search_v1` (search share only, the like-for-like check). Design selection, placebo test and threshold are unchanged from ADR-0034/0038. Review notebook: `reports/methods_annex/05_brand_index_factor.ipynb`.
- **Result:**
  - **Validity screen.** Google share of search passes: levels 0.45 / 0.57 and changes 0.29 / 0.26 with engagement / consideration. Wikipedia share fails on changes (+0.03 / −0.16) and is used only in the sensitivity.
  - **Model fit.** All loadings are positive: search share 2.04, engagement 0.76, consideration 1.10; φ = 0.94. The survey fits at 0.48 / 0.59. The rival set barely matters: PC makers only versus the base pool shift the share by 1–2%.
  - **Index path.** About 100 before the announcement, 104 in 2025H1, 114 in 2025Q3 and 118 in 2026Q2; post-announcement mean 109.4. Removing the survey leaves the weekly path almost unchanged (correlation 0.9998), so the survey corroborates rather than drives it. Before the announcement, share of search and the survey barely co-move (−0.05 / +0.06 over 12 quarters).
  - **Total effect, factor index.** **+7.5** (≈ +7.5% brand share) over 100 weeks; synthetic DiD with 13 base-plus-sensitivity donors; leave-one-out +7.0 to +9.9; **placebo p = 0.071**. That is the smallest attainable p with 13 placebos: Lenovo diverges more than every rival, in all three windows. Excluding the spring 2026 surge the effect is +6.1; before it, +5.3. All 360 specifications are positive.
  - **Like-for-like check (share of search only).** +7.2; p = 0.074 with 26 placebos; Xiaomi diverges more. p = 0.111 excluding the surge and 0.222 before it.
  - **Profile over time.** Most of the gap arises from 2025Q3 onward: about +2 in 2025H1, +11 in 2025Q3 and +16 in 2026Q2.
  - **Canonical headline.** +0.91 points (≈ +9.6% search salience), p = 0.111.
- **Consequences:** The brand-level index gives a sponsorship effect of the same direction and similar size as the canonical headline: about +7% brand share against about +10% search salience. Its significance is clearer on the factor index's selected design, but fragile on the share-only check once the spring 2026 surge weeks are removed. The canonical index and headline (ADR-0038) are unchanged until the owner decides whether this index replaces them. If it does, the valuation chain, which is calibrated to the canonical index's points, must be re-derived before reuse. Both configs are in `srmp.pipeline` DERIVED.

- **Consequences:**
  - **Reporting.** Stakeholder reporting quotes "about +7% brand share of attention, borderline significant", with the former headline shown as a cross-check, never the improved p-value alone.
  - **Brand value.** It is now market-cap based, and the choice between the literal (US$446m) and the Lenovo-specific (US$5.0bn) reading is an owner decision; the DCF figures in ADR-0033 and ADR-0038 are historical.
  - **Units.** Earlier ADRs' Brand Index points (100/15 scale) are not comparable with current points.
  - **Dashboard.** The dashboard data script (`frontend/scripts/extract_real_data.py`) reads `brand_index_weekly.csv` and will pick up the new index when regenerated; it was not regenerated (dashboard out of scope).

## ADR-0041 - Retire the multi-signal index comparison; the monetisation gate reads the headline

- **Context:** After ADR-0040 two loose ends remained:
  - `srmp/index/multisignal_compare.py` still ran in the pipeline. It compared Lenovo-only variants of the superseded search-salience index (evidence-weighted, PCA and entropy weights), and no analysis read its outputs.
  - `sponsorship_monetization_v1`'s counterfactual gate still read the original 2024 design (`sponsorship_counterfactual_v1`, on the search-salience index) rather than the headline total effect.

  The owner asked for both to be closed in the interest of tidiness and clarity.
- **Decision:**
  - **Retirement.** `multisignal_compare` is retired: the module, its pipeline step, its test, its outputs (`data/curated/index/brand_index_method_comparison_*`) and its figure are removed. Its annex stays as a historical record and the code remains in git history. Signal weighting is now done by the factor model's loadings and the P4 validity screen (ADR-0039).
  - **Gate input.** The monetisation counterfactual gate reads `sponsorship_total_effect_v1/estimate_manifest.json`: candidate = the total effect in Brand Index points; status = whether it passes the pre-registered placebo test; and the royalty reference index = the mean synthetic level. `use_counterfactual_candidate` stays `false`, so even a passing headline is used only after explicit owner approval.
  - **Pipeline order.** The pipeline now runs the total-effect experiments before monetisation, and monetisation before the original 2024 design (which reads its adstock).
- **Result:** The gate reports `passes_primary_placebo_test`: lift +7.48, p = 0.071, not used for valuation. The valuation remains blocked on the royalty schedule, fee, revenue base, discount rate and persistence horizon.
- **Consequences:** Every experiment in `srmp/experiments/` now has a current review notebook and the methods-annex README lists no open documentation actions; the search-salience cross-check index is documented by its July method annex plus the current comparisons in notebooks 05 and 10.

## ADR-0042 - Exposure-timing diagnostic: FIFA against Lenovo's other sponsorships

- **Context:** The headline total effect (ADR-0038, ADR-0040) compares Lenovo with rivals since the FIFA announcement. It does not separate FIFA from Lenovo's other sponsorships.
  - **Other sponsorships.** Ducati (technology partner since 2018, title partner since 2021), MotoGP (Dorna technical partner since May 2019), F1 (Official Partner since the 2022 season, Global Partner with two title races from 2025, announced September 2024) and the Carolina Hurricanes' Lenovo Center (September 2024).
  - **Their scale.** Their Blinkfire digital exposure exceeded FIFA's in most quarters before the World Cup.
  - **Owner's hypothesis.** Sponsorships that pre-date FIFA are part of the pre-period the synthetic control was fitted on, so only exposure above their usual level, plus FIFA, is incremental. Test it by extending their exposure backward to before Blinkfire coverage.
- **Decision:** New diagnostic experiment `sponsorship_exposure_timing_v1`. It never changes the headline.
  - **Back-cast.** For each pre-existing property, motorsport exposure from 2022 to September 2024 is constructed: the property's observed ISO-week Blinkfire profile in its first year (October 2024 to September 2025), scaled each year by its own co-brand Google search level ("Lenovo F1", "Lenovo MotoGP", "Lenovo Ducati"; observed since 2022). Co-brand search after June 2025 is not used, because of the 2025Q3 Google low-volume change.
  - **Baseline.** The pre-FIFA baseline is that profile at the 2022–2024 mean intensity.
  - **Other properties.** Properties without a pre-sample activation (Hurricanes, Canadiens, Yankees) count as fully incremental.
  - **Regressions.** The weekly headline gap is regressed on standardised log adstock (δ = 0.80) with Lenovo event controls and Newey–West errors. Three tests: (1) before FIFA, back-cast motorsport exposure; (2) after the announcement, FIFA exposure and non-FIFA exposure above its baseline; (3) the full sample. Each is run in levels without and with a trend and in weekly changes; the full sample also gets a trend plus an after-announcement step, so that FIFA's coefficient comes from within-sponsorship variation.
  - **Wiring.** Pipeline step after the total-effect runs; reviewed in `10_total_sponsorship_effect.ipynb` §5.7; tests in `tests/test_exposure_timing.py`.
- **Result (exposure through 2026-07-20):**
  - **Pre-existing exposure is absorbed.** Before FIFA, motorsport exposure does not move the gap with a trend (−0.01 points per s.d., p 0.97) or week to week (+0.04, p 0.62). It does without a trend (+0.64, p 0.01), but that is a shared drift.
  - **Non-FIFA exposure rose after the announcement:** MotoGP 1.11×, F1 1.41× and Ducati 1.99× their pre-FIFA baselines.
  - **It doesn't explain the gap.** Non-FIFA exposure is never positively associated with the gap in a specification with a trend: post-period incremental −2.04 (p 0.005); full sample +0.06 (p 0.86).
  - **FIFA exposure does, in levels.** Full sample with trend and step: **+6.29 points per s.d. (p 0.001)**; with trend only, +2.20 (p 0.001).
  - **But not week to week, and not against a post-period trend.** Weekly changes: +0.12 (p 0.20). With a trend fitted on the post-period alone: −1.26 (p 0.45). FIFA log adstock correlates 0.78 with time after the announcement.
- **Consequences:**
  - **The hypothesis is supported.** Lenovo's pre-existing sponsorships are absorbed in the pre-period fit, and neither they nor their post-2024 increase (including the F1 upgrade) explain the gap.
  - **The evidence points to FIFA.** The gap builds with accumulated FIFA exposure, which supports reading the headline as a FIFA effect more than an effect of Lenovo's other sponsorships.
  - **It is not a dose-response proof.** The gap does not respond to week-to-week exposure, so a cumulative brand-building effect and a slow Lenovo-specific drift remain observationally close.
  - **Assumptions to state alongside the result.** The back-cast is constructed and the exposure is digital only.

## ADR-0043 - The FIFA-specific effect is the primary sponsorship estimate

- **Context:** ADR-0042 showed that the gap between Lenovo and its no-sponsorship twin builds with accumulated FIFA exposure. Lenovo's other sponsorships, already in the pre-period fit, do not explain it. The owner asked for the FIFA-specific part of the gap, with whatever is unexplained left as a residual.
- **Decision:**
  - **Primary estimate.** The project's primary sponsorship estimate is the **FIFA-specific effect**. The mean post-announcement gap is decomposed with the full-sample regression from ADR-0042, in levels with a linear trend (fixed in `config/experiments/sponsorship_exposure_timing_v1.yaml`, `fifa_specific_effect.primary_specification`).
  - **Components.** FIFA part = FIFA-exposure coefficient × mean post-period standardised FIFA log adstock, measured from zero exposure. Other sponsorships = their coefficient × exposure above the pre-FIFA baseline. Events = their coefficients × post-period frequency. Residual = the rest (trend, intercept, unexplained).
  - **Bands.** (i) Statistical: FIFA part ± 1.96 × its Newey–West standard error. (ii) Attribution upper: the whole gap over the same weeks, i.e. the residual also credited to FIFA.
  - **Why this specification.** The other levels specifications give FIFA anywhere from −7.7 to +18.8 points: they either extrapolate from zero exposure (negative residual) or let a trend fitted on the post-period alone absorb FIFA's cumulative build-up. The specification was fixed after the decomposition was seen.
  - **Total effect.** It stays computed as the base of the decomposition and is reported as the upper attribution band over the full post period.
  - **Valuation.** `brand_value_dominance_v1` adds `fifa_specific_incremental_brand_value` (primary uplift and bands, literal and Lenovo-specific readings). The total-effect chain stays as the upper band; `sponsorship_total_return_v3` and `valuation_routes_v1` still use the total effect as comparison routes.
  - **Documentation.** `10_total_sponsorship_effect.ipynb` §5.8 and its interpretation lead with the FIFA-specific effect; `40_brand_value.ipynb` §5.4.
- **Result (92 weeks with Blinkfire coverage, 2024-10-21 to 2026-07-20; twin level 102.0):**
  - **FIFA-specific effect: +4.42 Brand Index points ≈ +4.3% brand share of attention**, with a 95% band of +1.70 to +7.14 (+1.7% to +7.0%).
  - **Rest of the gap:** other sponsorships +0.02, events −0.19, residual +2.78.
  - **Attribution upper band:** +7.02 over the same weeks (+6.9%). The total effect over 100 weeks is +7.48 (+7.3%).
  - **Incremental brand value, literal reading:** **US$19.3m** (95% band 7.4 to 31.2; attribution upper 30.7).
  - **Incremental brand value, Lenovo-specific reading:** **US$218m** (84 to 353; upper 347).
- **Consequences:**
  - **Reporting.** Stakeholder reporting quotes "about +4% brand share from FIFA (band +2% to +7%)", with the total effect as the ceiling.
  - **Inherited caveats.** The estimate inherits the total effect's borderline significance and the ADR-0042 assumptions: back-cast motorsport exposure, digital-only exposure, log adstock as the exposure measure.
  - **Selection after seeing results.** The primary specification was chosen after the decomposition was seen, so the specification table must accompany it.
  - **Unchanged.** The World Cup close-up (notebook 20) and the comparison routes still read the total effect.
